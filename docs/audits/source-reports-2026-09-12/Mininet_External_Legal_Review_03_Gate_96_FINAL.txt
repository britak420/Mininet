2. WHY THE CURRENT "NO SELLER" FRAMING IS NOT ENOUGH
======================================================================

CURRENT SOURCE POSITION

SPEC-07 currently says:

  * anyone may send BTC or XMR into a community-controlled treasury;
  * a verified deposit causes MINI issuance at a governance-set rate;
  * the rate is the "day-one price";
  * incoming BTC/XMR strengthens the commons/reserve;
  * there is "no seller and no contract";
  * the mechanism is intended to be live from block 1.

The Whitepaper likewise describes the external-asset contribution path as a
way for wealthy participants to acquire extra MINI and describes external
assets as lifting/strengthening holdings.

LEGAL ANALYSIS

The automatic nature of a transaction does not eliminate its economic
substance.

A person provides external value.
The system issues a different crypto-asset in response at a stated rate.

Under U.S. FinCEN guidance, labels such as "earning," "creating," "purchasing,"
or similar terms are not controlling; facts and circumstances determine whether
a person is an exchanger/administrator/money transmitter.

Under securities/consumer/financial-promotion regimes, the economic
transaction and communications matter more than the project's chosen label.

Therefore:

  "contribution" cannot be used as a synonym for "not a sale."

The strongest way to preserve the constitutional "no seller" premise is not to
argue over terminology. It is to remove the primary sale/exchange fact:

  no external asset is paid to the project/protocol for newly issued MINI.

3. U.S. SECURITIES ANALYSIS
======================================================================

3.1 Current SEC framework reviewed

The SEC/CFTC March 2026 final interpretation restates Howey as:

  investment of money
  in a common enterprise
  with a reasonable expectation of profits
  derived from essential managerial efforts of others.

It also states that administrative/ministerial tasks are not enough to satisfy
the essential-managerial-efforts element.

The same 2026 interpretation describes "digital commodities" as crypto-assets
intrinsically linked to a functional crypto system whose value derives from
programmatic operation and supply/demand rather than essential managerial
efforts; it describes decentralization in terms of no person/entity/group
having operational, economic, or voting control.

It also emphasizes an important separation:
a non-security crypto asset itself can still be offered/sold subject to an
investment contract depending on the transaction/promises surrounding it.

3.2 Present Mininet risk

CURRENT VERDICT: FAIL / HIGH RISK under the existing SPEC-07 launch wording.

Mechanisms causing risk:

  * external assets paid in exchange for newly issued MINI;
  * an express governed conversion rate;
  * "day-one price";
  * statements that contributed external assets strengthen/lift holdings;
  * early founder/developer work can be perceived as essential to future
    network/value;
  * promised immediate liquidity through XRPL;
  * public emphasis on wealthy contributors acquiring extra MINI.

This does not prove a court would find an investment contract.
It creates facts the project does not need.

3.3 Approved U.S. posture

POST-REMEDIATION VERDICT: PASS as the launch design.

Mininet launches only after its production network is functional.

There is:
  no token sale;
  no BTC/XMR-for-MINI issuance;
  no promise of profit;
  no passive yield;
  no claim on an entity;
  no founder allocation;
  no official liquidity promise;
  no essential managerial-performance contract;
  no project-run redemption.

MINI's intended value is tied to use in a running protocol and supply/demand.

This is deliberately designed toward the 2026 SEC's non-security native
crypto-asset/digital-commodity fact pattern.

IMPORTANT:
The official Mininet documentation must NOT say:
  "the SEC has determined MINI is a digital commodity."

No regulator has made a MINI-specific determination.

3.4 Protocol rewards

The 2026 SEC interpretation concludes covered protocol-mining rewards are
payments for participants' protocol work rather than profits derived from
others' managerial efforts.

Mininet is not proof-of-work, so that exact safe category cannot simply be
copied.

The useful principle is narrower:

  reward actual protocol activity under predetermined software rules;
  do not reward passive token ownership or investment in a promoter.

That is the approved reward framing.

3.5 Human share

The SEC's 2026 airdrop interpretation is specifically limited to certain
non-security crypto assets where recipients provide no money, goods, services
or other consideration in exchange.

Mininet should NOT claim the human share automatically falls into that precise
airdrop category, because personhood/presence conditions have their own facts.

Instead, the legal-risk controls are:

  * no money is paid for the human share;
  * no promoter receives consideration;
  * it is the equal constitutional distribution mechanism;
  * it vests slowly;
  * it is available under general human eligibility rather than investor
    selection;
  * it buys no governance weight;
  * no appreciation is promised.

4. U.S. FINCEN / MONEY-TRANSMISSION ANALYSIS
======================================================================

CURRENT SPEC-07 VERDICT: FAIL / HIGH.

FinCEN's longstanding CVC framework distinguishes users from exchangers and
administrators and treats money-transmitter status as a facts-and-circumstances
question.

FinCEN also states that software development/distribution by itself is not
money transmission, even if the software facilitates CVC activity.

But a developer/operator can become a money transmitter when it actually uses
or deploys the system as a business to accept/transmit value for others.

The current SPEC-07 path:
  receives BTC into treasury custody
  and issues a different CVC at a stated rate.

That is too close to exchange/administration activity to treat "autonomous
code" as a reliable launch defense.

APPROVED POSTURE: PASS.

At genesis:

  * developers publish software;
  * users self-custody;
  * protocol transfers are P2P;
  * no project person/business accepts external assets in order to issue/sell
    MINI;
  * no project-run hosted wallet;
  * no project-run exchange/onramp;
  * no project-run user money transmission.

An independent business that later performs exchange/custody/transmission must
analyze/register/license itself where required.

The core must not provide a special legal privilege to that business.

No project-run external-asset contribution treasury.
No official project exchanger/custodian.

Final decision:
direct XMR custody/contribution is not a Mininet genesis service.

PROTOCOL CORE: PASS.

It does not become the network's identity provider.

6. EUROPEAN UNION — MiCA AND TRANSFER-OF-FUNDS
======================================================================

6.1 MiCA offer risk

MiCA generally imposes obligations on a person making a public offer of
crypto-assets other than ARTs/EMTs, subject to exemptions/exclusions.

Article 4 contains exclusions including certain offers for free, certain
ledger-maintenance/validation rewards, and specified utility-token cases.

MiCA also makes clear that the free-offer treatment does not apply where an
offeror receives fees, commissions, monetary or non-monetary benefits in
exchange for the crypto-asset.

That makes the present external-asset "contribution" mechanism a poor fit for
a free-distribution posture.

MiCA also requires applicable marketing information to be fair, clear and not
misleading and prohibits assertions about future value in the regulated
white-paper context.

6.2 No identifiable issuer / decentralized operation

A 2026 ESMA Q&A carrying a European Commission answer states that where
crypto-assets have no identifiable issuer, Title II's white-paper obligation
does not apply in the specified circumstances; CASPs serving such assets remain
regulated.

Another ESMA Q&A notes that fully decentralised services without an
intermediary may fall outside MiCA on a case-by-case basis.

APPROVED MININET APPROACH:

  * do not create an identifiable support organization that sells/offers MINI;
  * do not have the support organization seek admission to trading;
  * make the network operational/autonomous;
  * do not promise future value;
  * do not use "issuerless" as a fiction where a real offeror exists.

6.3 EU CASP boundary

A Mininet support organization does NOT at genesis provide:

  custody/admin of users' crypto;
  exchange crypto for funds;
  exchange crypto for other crypto;
  execute orders;
  place crypto-assets;
  receive/transmit client orders;
  provide transfer services for clients;
  operate a trading platform.

Independent EU CASPs can support MINI under their own authorization.

6.4 EU Transfer of Funds Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 applies where a CASP is involved in covered
crypto-asset transfers.

It expressly excludes person-to-person transfers carried out without a CASP.

It also distinguishes ancillary infrastructure/software developers from the
regulated service provider where they do not themselves perform transfers.

APPROVED CORE:
self-custodial P2P protocol.

No central Mininet CASP is necessary.

6.5 Stablecoin / asset-referenced-token risk

MINI must not purport to maintain a stable value by reference to external
assets or currencies.

No redemption against external treasury assets.

The term "reserve" must not be used to imply collateral backing.

If external assets are ever held by a community mechanism, call them
"community external assets" or similarly neutral language and state that MINI
holders have no redemption/property right in them.

Support donations are unconditional support of that separate organization.

No token entitlement.

banking/payments as legally required.
